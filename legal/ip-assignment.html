IP & ownership

Short version: you own it. This page explains the mechanism, because "you own your data" is a sentence anyone can write.

1. Your idea

You own your Submission entirely. Nothing transfers to us. The only right we receive is a limited, revocable, non-sublicensable, non-transferable licence to use it solely to perform the services you asked for, for only as long as we are performing them. NDA §3.1.

That licence is drawn narrowly on purpose. The broad grants common elsewhere — perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, sublicensable, transferable — are each doing specific work, and together they mean the grant outlives your relationship with the company. Ours expires when we stop working for you.

2. What gets built

You own all Work Product: source code, configuration, documentation, copy, designs, brand assets, and the operating data generated by what we build.

2.1 The mechanism, in two layers

Ownership language often fails on a technicality, so the NDA uses belt and braces:

  • Work made for hire. To the extent Work Product qualifies, it is a work made for hire for you, meaning you are the author from the outset.
  • Present assignment fallback. Work-made-for-hire doctrine only reaches certain categories of work, and software often falls outside them. So to the extent it does not qualify, we hereby assign all right, title, and interest to you at the moment of creation. The present tense matters: "hereby assigns" transfers now, whereas "agrees to assign" is only a promise to sign something later, which is precisely what a company in distress cannot be relied on to do.

NDA §4.2.

3. What we keep, and the licence you get to it

We retain our own platform, infrastructure, and generally applicable tooling — the things that are not specific to you.

Where any such component is embedded in your Work Product, you get a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free licence to it, to the extent needed to use, modify, and exploit that Work Product without us. NDA §4.3.

Without that clause, "you own the code" can still mean the code does not run once you leave, because some load-bearing piece belongs to the platform. Ownership of something that only works on our infrastructure is not ownership; it is a subscription with extra steps.

4. What we do not take

  • No equity in your venture.
  • No royalty, commission, or success fee.
  • No percentage of your revenue — we are never in your payment path at all.
  • No security interest in your assets.
  • No right of first refusal on your next round, sale, or anything else.
  • No licence to your trade marks beyond displaying them in the product we run for you.

NDA §4.4.

5. Leaving

Export everything, any time, free, in a non-proprietary machine-readable format: repository, database, assets, configuration, and domain records.

Export survives cancellation for at least 90 days and is never placed behind a payment — including where we terminated you for non-payment. NDA §10.

We also hold ourselves to the practical version of this: what you export should run. An export that produces an unbuildable archive satisfies the letter of a portability promise and none of its purpose.

6. What ownership does not get you

Stated plainly, because the rest of this page is good news and you should not read it as more than it is:

  • Owning your idea does not mean it is patented. If it is patentable, file — talk to an attorney about a provisional application.
  • Owning your idea does not stop independent invention by someone who never saw it. No agreement can do that.
  • Owning your code does not make it novel. If we build you something obvious, you own something obvious.
  • Ownership is only worth what you can enforce. Our contribution is to make sure the paperwork is not the weak link.
